JACS
2020 Research Article
Biphasic Bioelectrocatalytic Synthesis of Chiral β-Hydroxy Nitriles
Fangyuan Dong, Hui Chen, Christian A. Malapit, Matthew B. Prater, Min Li, Mengwei Yuan, Koun Lim, Shelley D. Minteer
A biphasic bioelectrocatalytic system was designed for the asymmetric synthesis of chiral β-hydroxy nitriles using alcohol dehydrogenase (AdhS) and halohydrin dehalogenase (HHDH). Two key obstacles in oxidoreductase-based synthesis were addressed: the consumption of high stoichiometric amounts of reduced cofactor, and the low solubility of organic substrates in the aqueous phase. Addition of methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE) as an organic phase increased substrate loading, prevented spontaneous hydrolysis, and improved Faradaic efficiency. After 10 h, the system produced 25.5 mM chiral product with 81.2% enantiomeric excess and 85% conversion — 8.8× higher than the single-phase system.
DOI: 10.1021/jacs.0c01890 ↗

Cell Reports Physical Science
2021 Research Article
An Engineered, Non-Diazotrophic Cyanobacterium and Its Application in Bioelectrochemical Nitrogen Fixation
Fangyuan Dong, Yoo Seok Lee, Erin M. Gaffney, Matteo Grattieri, Helena Haddadin, Hui Chen, Shelley D. Minteer
Microbial nitrogen fixation offers a promising route to ammonia production under mild conditions. This work reports an engineered, non-diazotrophic Synechococcus elongatus PCC 7942 strain with nitrogen fixation activity, constructed by integrating a modified nitrogenase gene cluster into the genome. The engineered strain was employed in a bioelectrochemical nitrogen-fixation (e-BNF) system. After 48 h, the e-BNF system accumulated 173 μM NH₃ — 21× higher than photosynthesis-driven nitrogen fixation alone — with a Faradaic efficiency of 6.85%.
DOI: 10.1016/j.xcrp.2021.100444 ↗

ACS Catalysis
2021 Research Article
Engineering Cyanobacterium with Transmembrane Electron Transfer Ability for Bioelectrochemical Nitrogen Fixation
Fangyuan Dong, Olja Simoska, Erin M. Gaffney, Shelley D. Minteer
A genetic strategy was developed to engineer Synechococcus elongatus PCC 7942 with transmembrane electron transfer (TET) ability for mediator-free bioelectrochemical nitrogen fixation (e-BNF). By engineering electron conduits across the cell membrane, the system eliminates the need for soluble redox mediators — a key barrier to practical e-BNF implementation. The approach demonstrates how synthetic biology tools can be combined with bioelectrochemistry to overcome fundamental limitations in microbial electrosynthesis.
DOI: 10.1021/acscatal.1c03038 ↗

Electrochemical Science Advances
2022 Review
Applying Synthetic Biology Strategies to Bioelectrochemical Systems
Fangyuan Dong, Olja Simoska, Erin M. Gaffney, Shelley D. Minteer
This review discusses the use of synthetic biology to engineer non-native properties into bioelectrochemical systems (BES) — including microbial fuel cells, electrosynthesis platforms, and biosensors. Two primary strategies are explored: engineering organisms for expanded fuel utilization and novel electrosynthetic pathways, and improving the abiotic/biotic interface to enhance performance. The review argues that combining synthetic biology with materials innovation is necessary to achieve commercially viable BES, and outlines the most promising directions for the field.
DOI: 10.1002/elsa.202100197 ↗

PhD Thesis
2022 Doctoral Dissertation
Designing Bioelectrosynthesis System for Sustainable Production of Value-Added Chemicals
Fangyuan Dong — University of Utah, Department of Chemistry
This dissertation presents the design and development of bioelectrosynthesis systems that couple electrochemical energy input with enzymatic and microbial catalysis for the sustainable production of value-added chemicals. Key contributions include: a biphasic bioelectrocatalytic platform for the asymmetric synthesis of chiral β-hydroxy nitriles; the construction of an engineered, non-diazotrophic cyanobacterium capable of bioelectrochemical nitrogen fixation; and the engineering of transmembrane electron transfer pathways to enable mediator-free microbial electrosynthesis. Together, these works advance the frontier of bioelectrochemistry at the bio/abiotic interface, bridging synthetic biology and electrochemical energy conversion.
University of Utah, 2022 ↗
